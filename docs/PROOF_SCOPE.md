# Native Lean proof scope

The theorems below import native Lean definitions. `lake build Tornado` kernel-checks them with pinned Lean 4.29.1. `#print axioms` is emitted for every theorem; the verifier accepts only Lean's standard `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`. There are no custom axioms, `sorry`, or `admit` in the compiled proof modules. The runnable CLI exposes read-only routes and explicitly experimental deposit, one-note 4337/Classic-relayer withdrawal, batch-withdrawal and recovery send adapters. The pure money-path policy theorems are not end-to-end proofs of these runnable adapters. Approved C OS/HTTPS/signing boundaries and the installed worker executable remain trusted. This is best-effort verification, not a guarantee of bug-free software or fund safety.

## Finite catalog and plan validation

`CatalogProofs.lean` proves that the static catalog has 29 entries, every amount is positive, mainnet has 20 entries, Sepolia has nine, and addresses are unique. This checks the **Lean constants**; the frozen TypeScript oracle and parity vectors provide the external correspondence evidence.

`Plan.lean` defines `validatePlan`, which the executable runs on each printed choice. Its theorems prove that a successful validator result implies the returned plan has the requested exact value. The validator also checks nonempty entries, positive counts, allowed pools, total note count, and batch count. This does not prove that branch-and-bound always finds an optimal plan; the 1,860-case grid gives bounded differential evidence only.

## Native policy

`PolicyProofs.lean` proves that simulation admission has the reviewed normalized UserOperation hash and the source's upper age bound, and that an explicit failed result cannot pass. The source age predicate has no lower bound, so a future timestamp can pass; the theorem preserves that behavior rather than silently strengthening it.

The relayer fee theorems prove that a returned result passed the selected amount/cap guards, its fields equal the native arithmetic, and `net + fee = amount`. Proof, pool, endpoint, relayer registration, and HTTP response validation are absent, so this is not a full relayer withdrawal theorem.

The direct one-note policy theorems prove that signing binds the reviewed hash; reservation copies that signed hash; exposure and the single send require a matching reservation; a submitted candidate cannot send again; unknown blocks retry; and confirmation requires a successful receipt plus matching EntryPoint and pool Withdrawal event flags. The policy can also mark an exposed candidate unknown or reconcile it from exact evidence if a simulation RPC relays it before an explicit send is recorded; exposure never permits a fresh signature or retry. These event flags are inputs to the pure policy. A separate decoder checks log bytes and typed receipt metadata, but the receipt is not authenticated against chain consensus or a durable journal. These are policy properties, not a proof about a real transaction.

`Status.lean` decodes and validates a subset of the captured one-note status JSON schema. Its theorems show that an existing signed-candidate list blocks replacement even with the legacy retry flag and that a confirmed candidate takes aggregation priority. The CLI uses protected file reads and compare-and-replace for one-note status, but those filesystem effects and actual process restart behavior are not proved by these theorems.

`BatchProofs.lean` proves that the pure batch gate accepts only a nonempty, distinct selection of at most 12 notes, binds the signed hash to review, permits a reservation only for a selected unreserved note, requires all selected notes to be reserved before exposure and send, prevents a second send or replacement signing after exposure, and demands receipt/EntryPoint success plus exact selected-note event coverage to mark confirmation. A `reserveNote` step assumes a successful durable write. Concrete protected journals have fault tests, but no theorem connects all their IO effects to this model. The theorem cannot establish storage durability or prevent a hostile caller from supplying false chain evidence.

## Native crypto and ABI boundaries

`Crypto/Properties.lean` proves selected fail-closed input checks: empty or wrong-size key material, invalid EIP-712 chain/nonce bounds, and out-of-range pool addresses are rejected by the native functions. These are useful input-domain facts; they do not prove SHA, Keccak, BIP39, BIP32, secp256k1, EIP-712, MiMC, or Pedersen equivalent to independent specifications on all inputs. The fixed compatibility vectors provide sampled behavioral evidence only. The current secp256k1 and field arithmetic is variable-time and does not zeroize secrets.

`Protocol/Abi.lean` proves that accepted contract-call arrays meet the 12-call cap and that every accepted canonical sponsor tuple decoder result re-encodes to the exact full input bytes. Viem vectors check selected ERC-20, Classic pool, SimpleAccount, ordered call-hash, and paymaster tuple encodings. `ImportedAbi.lean` adds bounded Safe Transaction Builder method arguments, nested tuples and arrays, checked by finite viem vectors. These theorems do not establish arbitrary imported-ABI equivalence or EIP-4337 transaction correctness.

`Protocol/Events.lean` strictly decodes the pinned pool Deposit/Withdrawal and EntryPoint UserOperationEvent shapes. Its theorems establish that a matching Withdrawal has the expected emitting pool and fields, and that direct or batch confirmation requires the corresponding exact decoded events and caller-supplied successful receipt. Seven viem vectors and malformed/spoofed-log cases test the decoder. The caller-supplied receipt and logs are not authenticated by this module, and it does not establish chain finality or connect to the policy transitions.

`Protocol/UserOperation08.lean` proves that the native EntryPoint v0.8 hash ignores the account signature and the contents of an already-present separate paymaster signature, that an absent paymaster produces empty packed bytes, and that uint128 gas overflow is rejected. Nine pinned viem vectors exercise the typed-data hash, short/padded EIP-7702 marker transport differences, factory and paymaster packing, and the optional paymaster signature marker. These do not prove the deployed EntryPoint, paymaster, bundler, or signer path.

`Protocol/Rlp.lean` proves three narrow encoding guards for EIP-1559/7702 and checks 20 viem vectors. `TypedTransaction.lean` and `TypedTransactionDecode.lean` sign and canonically decode EIP-1559/7702 payloads and check 43 pinned viem cases. `SetCodeValidity.lean` adds a strict stateless envelope/intent gate beyond viem serializer compatibility; nine theorems capture accepted shape, intrinsic gas and effective authorization guards. None of these establishes account state, nonce freshness, fee adequacy, or chain acceptance.

`Protocol/Groth16R1CS.lean` checks a complete witness against all 28,275 pinned Tornado constraints and proves the individual row/index and equation checks. `WitnessVM.lean` now generates all 28,300 slots in native Lean and authenticates the decoded R1CS before returning a checked witness. Four public cases pass, one matching every SDK witness slot. Five theorems cover selected bit/mux properties and the final checked-witness gate. A separate public fixture produces exact proof bytes, but these theorems do not prove proof-generation correctness or Circom-to-Lean equivalence for every possible input. See [Groth16 scope](GROTH16_SCOPE.md).

`Protocol/TornadoWitness.lean` computes the first 49 input slots natively, after checking note preimages, the nullifier hash, a 20-level MiMC path, and a caller-supplied expected root. Four theorems cover prefix length and selected slot positions, and a pinned full-witness fixture checks all 49 slots. `WitnessVM.lean` fills the remaining slots as described above. The expected root is not authenticated against chain history here; see [witness scope](WITNESS_SCOPE.md).

`Protocol/Groth16Key.lean` rejects any key header whose dimensions or section offsets differ from the pinned proving key and proves that a successful parse satisfies its layout predicate. `Protocol/ProvingKey.lean` authenticates the full uncompressed digest and parses every key section, validating G1 curve and G2 twist equations, canonical field encodings, and polynomial bounds. It does not establish G2 subgroup membership, trusted-setup security, or proof production; see [key scope](PROVING_KEY_SCOPE.md).

`Protocol/BN254G1.lean` implements bounded base-field arithmetic and G1 Jacobian point operations. Four theorems prove field-result bounds and that an accepted inverse passed its executable multiplicative check. Fourteen pinned noble scalar/addition vectors test its point arithmetic. These facts do not prove the group law, constant-time behavior, pairing validity, or complete Groth16 proof production; see [G1 scope](BN254_G1_SCOPE.md).

`Protocol/BN254G2.lean` adds quadratic-extension and twist G2 arithmetic. Fourteen pinned noble scalar/addition vectors and a generator-order check pass, but no arbitrary-point subgroup theorem exists; see [G2 scope](BN254_G2_SCOPE.md). `BN254PairingField.lean`, `BN254TatePairing.lean`, and `BN254Groth16Verify.lean` add a public-point reduced Tate pairing and the Classic four-pair equation. Seven theorems cover executable inverse, output-order, and signal-bound guards. A pinned full-size public proof passes and all six changed signals fail. They do not establish extension-field laws, Miller-loop correctness, Groth16 soundness, or optimal-Ate correspondence; see [pairing scope](BN254_PAIRING_STATUS.md).

`Protocol/ClassicVerifyingKey.lean` compiles the complete Classic key into Lean and exposes a checked wrapper without a caller-key argument. Two theorems show that a successful result used the checked key and canonical public signals. Offline tests compare every coordinate against a vendored immutable Solidity source through the pinned fixture. This does not prove deployed pool/verifier binding or pairing correctness; see [compiled key scope](CLASSIC_KEY_SCOPE.md).

The pinned CLI and SDK catalogs list pool addresses and denominations but no expected deployed pool runtime hashes. Their `eth_getCode` use establishes code presence only; the one pinned CLI code hash belongs to the unrelated Simple7702 implementation. A safe deployed-pool identity gate therefore needs an audited, source-derived manifest for the supported pool variants before this Lean port can compare exact code at a selected block. A caller-supplied hash would not fill that trust gap.

`Protocol/ClassicProofPipeline.lean` composes authenticated compressed assets, the complete checked witness, proof assembly, serialization, and compiled-key public verification. Its returned runtime object contains only public bytes and signals; the secret-bearing checked artifact exists in an erased proposition. Eight theorems bind the public result to reviewed signals and the executable witness, encoding, and verification gates. One unfunded public fixture matches the pinned SDK's exact 256 proof bytes. This does not prove Groth16 arithmetic or soundness for every input, secret-safe computation, or a deployed pool's verifier identity; see [pipeline scope](CLASSIC_PROOF_PIPELINE_SCOPE.md).

`Runtime/ProofBlinding.lean` samples nonzero canonical Fr values by bounded rejection from independent kernel CSPRNG reads. Its theorem proves the accepted candidate's 32-byte shape and field range, assuming the entropy callback returned those bytes. The internal `Cli/WithdrawProof.lean` reference builder and connected isolated prover use the sampler. The theorem does not prove randomness, independence, constant-time behavior, or zeroization; see [sampler scope](PROOF_BLINDING_SCOPE.md).

`Policy/EvidenceBridge.lean` composes the strict event decoder with the one-note and batch policy confirmation transitions. Its two theorems prove that an accepted bridge confirmation has exact decoded event evidence, a matching signed operation hash, the selected batch nullifiers where applicable, and an accepted native policy transition. The bridge still receives receipt status, logs, expected fields, and policy state from its caller; no RPC authenticity, finality, or durable state reachability is proved.

`Protocol/RpcReceipt.lean` parses typed transaction and bundler receipts, binds each event log to the receipt's transaction/block metadata, and gates confirmation on the exact expected events. Three theorems cover transaction binding for direct, batch, and batch-deposit outcomes. The HTTPS transport authenticates TLS to the configured server, not Ethereum consensus or finality; the receipt remains a server claim.

`Cli/SmartAccountStatus.lean` reads a protected recovery journal and gates an observed confirmation on a matching indexed zero-value type-2 transaction and receipt. Two theorems show that a `confirmed` result passed the transaction hash, sender, destination, calldata hash, selected-chain, and block-inclusion checks. The command now updates the journal through protected byte-for-byte compare-and-replace; those IO effects are not established by the pure theorems. The RPC can lie or reorganize; see [status scope](SMART_ACCOUNT_STATUS_SCOPE.md).

`Cli/NullifierSpent.lean` validates a selected catalog pool, chain, 32-byte nullifier hash, and numbered block before an exact-block `isSpent(bytes32)` RPC call. Its two theorems constrain accepted catalog selection and canonical true-word decoding. The RPC receives the nullifier before withdrawal and can link the query to the eventual spend. History/preflight and live withdrawal sessions perform spent checks; no chain truth or finality is proved. See [nullifier scope](NULLIFIER_SPENT_SCOPE.md).

`Protocol/SagaHistory.lean` proves accepted segment coverage reaches beyond the requested rewind. A partial Saga stream is allowed; the separate post-sync freshness check proves a caller-supplied selected-pool cursor reaches the captured head and has the selected chain and pool. `Protocol/RpcHistory.lean` proves generated `eth_getLogs` requests stay within the selected pool's registration and captured-head range. These are pure guards over supplied index/log bytes and RPC claims; they do not establish that an endpoint returned all chain events. See [native history scope](native-history-scope.md).

`Protocol/HistoryReducer.lean` replays selected-pool deposit and withdrawal events, rejects missing or repeated leaf indices and duplicate commitments/nullifiers, and compares a rebuilt MiMC root and count against same-block caller claims. Three theorems constrain the successful append and returned selected-pool identity/count/root postcondition. Six pinned SDK roots and adversarial replay cases test the implementation. The RPC claims and event completeness remain assumptions; see [reducer scope](HISTORY_REDUCER_SCOPE.md).

`Runtime/HistorySync.lean` composes a bounded Saga download, RPC log tails with range splitting, same-block pool calls, and the selected-pool reducer. Two theorems show that an accepted complete result reaches the captured head with the selected chain/pool and that the aggregate-byte predicate enforces its limit. Offline injected-transport tests cover fallback and faults. It serves `history`, `compliance report`, preflight and public withdrawal sessions. Its assets/cache adapter imports pinned local snapshots and persists protected content-addressed public history with checkpoint and reorg checks. These persistence effects are tested, not proved by the two sync theorems. Neither module proves provider completeness or finality; see [sync scope](HISTORY_SYNC_SCOPE.md) and [snapshot scope](HISTORY_SNAPSHOT_SCOPE.md).

`SmartAccount/Recovery.lean` validates pinned account code identity and supported recovery sweeps before emitting ordered native ABI calls. Its theorem says the accepted call data decodes back to those calls. It does not prove deployed account code or authorization state. `Runtime/JsonRpc.lean` rejects malformed JSON-RPC envelopes and noncanonical quantities; it is a parser, not a chain proof.

`Spec/Refinement.lean` supplies independent one-note and batch safety specifications and proves reachable native policy transitions refine selected reviewed-hash, reservation, send-cap and confirmation properties. `Spec/EffectRefinement.lean` additionally proves those properties for abstract one-note and batch command traces with durable-write acknowledgment and receipt-authentication inputs. The latter inputs are assumptions until a real IO adapter and chain authenticator are connected and proved.

`Runtime/AttemptJournal.lean` is a tested append-only IO adapter that replays those policy transitions from exclusive, synced private records and blocks sending after recovered exposure. `Runtime/NoteClaims.lean` wraps it with per-nullifier exclusive files inside one private instance root; two-process tests show one claimant wins. Neither module is a Lean theorem about actual disk durability or authenticated receipt evidence, and the abstract effect proofs are not yet refinements of this concrete IO implementation; see [journal scope](ATTEMPT_JOURNAL_SCOPE.md) and [claim scope](NOTE_CLAIMS_SCOPE.md).

Twelve additional theorems about the concrete pure replay and claim-name functions show that accepted direct and batch send events satisfy the policy gates, consume the send slot, and reject a second send, while distinct valid-prefix nullifier identifiers map to distinct claim filenames. They do not prove that the C binding durably wrote a record, that the IO replay loop matches an abstract fold after a crash, or that a network request follows the intent record.

`Spec/JournalIORefinement.lean` proves four properties over the public `replayRecord` step used by the IO journal loader. An accepted record has the exact attempt identity and sequence; accepted direct and batch send records require the corresponding reservations and consume their send slot; an immediate second send cannot replay. Process tests cover failed writes, competing arm attempts and restart after exposure. This is a refinement of the loader's pure replay step, not a proof of filesystem durability or network ordering; see [journal replay scope](JOURNAL_IO_REFINEMENT_SCOPE.md).

## Trust boundary and unproved properties

The Lean kernel, compiler, standard runtime, platform integer/byte-array behavior, and OS remain trusted. Crypto vector parity checks functional outputs for selected inputs but do not prove collision resistance, constant-time behavior, or secure randomness. The static catalog and test oracle are pinned to a particular TypeScript/SDK revision; deployed contracts are not proved to match it.

Connected experimental send adapters have protected journals and injected fault tests for review binding, reservation-before-exposure and stopping on ambiguity. There is no theorem that their actual C/HTTPS effects refine the abstract durable-reservation model. Exact recipient/pool/amount/fee and call ordering, signed-byte bindings, no blind retry and receipt/event checks are runtime properties with partial proof and test coverage. Secret-safe proving, authenticated worker/deployed-pool/verifier binding, consensus-state authentication and funded testnet validation are not established. The worker uses variable-time arithmetic and does not guarantee secret erasure. No real or funded transaction was performed during development; these results do not support an end-to-end production fund-safety claim.

The fund-safety specification is conditional on truthful chain evidence and
the expected contract, cryptographic and OS behavior: sign only reviewed
intent, preserve durable claims before executable exposure, reject duplicate
or replacement attempts under uncertainty, and confirm only exact transaction
and event evidence. The sections above identify which parts are proved and
which have runtime tests only. Bounded strict API parsing rejects malformed or
inconsistent answers, but cannot distinguish an internally consistent invented
chain from Ethereum without independent consensus-state authentication. Lean
proofs of acceptance rules do not authenticate HTTP replies.
